How to work:
1. Compare the readings field by field. Where they agree, keep the value (improving wording if needed).
2. Where they disagree, re-read the paper at the places their quotes point to, and decide on the evidence in the paper, not on either reader's authority.
3. Do NOT resolve a disagreement by taking the union. This applies especially to analytical_tasks and relevant_asjc_areas: include a value only if the paper shows the benchmark evaluates it. A shorter, defensible list beats a generous one.
4. The lenses have complementary blind spots. On what is verifiable in the text — exact benchmark name, dataset size, models, metrics, numbers, dates — prefer the reading better supported by its quotes, typically the technical one. On what needs disciplinary judgement — humanities_role, whether a task is interpretive or merely factual — weigh the humanist reading more, but still against the text.
5. humanities_role "both" requires distinct item groups or distinct reported metrics for content-as-data and for disciplinary competence; if the readings split between "both" and a specific value, default to the specific value unless the paper shows that evidence.
6. Facets (gold_*, answer_multiplicity, plurality_treatment) record how the benchmark was BUILT. Prefer "not_stated" over a plausible reconstruction; never split the difference; never let a facet be pulled into line with humanities_role or analytical_tasks — their divergence is informative.
7. Flags the supervisor could not resolve are listed with each reading: settle them if the paper allows, otherwise leave the field to the humans (see needs_human).
8. Enforce every controlled vocabulary in the final report, even where all readers agreed on an off-vocabulary value.
