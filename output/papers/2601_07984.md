## Benchmark Modalities

**Modality Types:** text, image

**Description:** Models are evaluated in a multimodal setting where they receive artwork images, culture tags, and text metadata to generate comprehensive art critiques.

## Analytical Tasks

**Task Types:** generation, interpretation, attribution, cultural reasoning, stylistic analysis, visual recognition

**Detailed Description:** The benchmark measures five progressive levels of analytical critique: L1 visual perception of pure forms (visual_recognition), L2 technical brushwork and medium analysis (stylistic_analysis), L3 iconographic cultural symbolism (interpretation), L4 art-historical context and origin (attribution), and L5 philosophical aesthetic concepts (cultural_reasoning and interpretation), all measured through free-text critiques (generation).

## Summary

This paper presents VULCA-BENCH, a tri-tier evaluation framework designed to assess VLM performance on cross-cultural expert-level art critique. By structuring cultural understanding across a five-level theoretical hierarchy and calibrating single-judge LLM outputs against human expert ratings, the authors systematically diagnose model gaps in cultural interpretation.

## Key Findings

The evaluation reveals that while VLMs generate fluent text and excel at surface-level visual descriptions, their performance degrades significantly on deeper cultural interpretation (Critique Depth and Cultural Alignment). Furthermore, under the calibrated framework, 13 of 15 models exhibit a strong Western-centric bias, consistently scoring higher on Western art than non-Western traditions.

**Facets Explanation:** The reference critiques and human calibrations were authored by domain experts across six distinct cultural traditions. The task requires external historical and philosophical knowledge not contained in the visual stimuli to perform deep cultural interpretation. Correctness is evaluated using community-dependent, culture-specific dimensions (e.g., 165 dimensions including Chinese painting theory and Western iconography). Multiplicity of interpretation is addressed using a 1-5 graded rubric scored by a single LLM judge, which is then calibrated against human expert scores. Annotator disagreement is reported as an inherent subjectivity challenge and treated as noise to be minimized via single-judge sigmoid calibration rather than preserved.
