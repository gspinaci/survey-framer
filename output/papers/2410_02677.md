## Benchmark Modalities

**Modality Types:** text

**Description:** The benchmark is entirely text-based, consisting of multiple-choice questions (CulturalBench-Easy) and binary True/False judgment questions for each option (CulturalBench-Hard) written in English.

## Analytical Tasks

**Task Types:** factual recall, multiple choice qa, fact verification, cultural reasoning

**Detailed Description:** Evaluates LLMs on factual recall of cultural norms (e.g., eating habits, traditional attire) via multiple-choice QA (CulturalBench-Easy) and binary fact verification (CulturalBench-Hard) to assess reasoning over culture-specific contexts, linguistic habits, and social etiquette.

## Summary

CulturalBench is a human-verified dataset of 1,696 questions designed to evaluate the cultural knowledge of LLMs across 45 global regions and 17 distinct cultural topics. Curated via a collaborative human-AI red-teaming pipeline called 'CulturalTeaming', it includes two settings: a multiple-choice 'Easy' benchmark and a 'Hard' multi-label binary evaluation. The dataset exposes significant cultural blind spots and mode-seeking limitations in contemporary LLMs.

## Key Findings

While models perform well on the multiple-choice version (up to 89.6%), they struggle significantly on CulturalBench-Hard, with the best model (OpenAI o1) reaching only 61.4% accuracy compared to a 92.4% human baseline. Models exhibit an average performance drop of 28.7% on questions with multiple valid answers due to their mode-seeking tendencies, and they consistently underperform on regions like South America, East Europe, and the Middle East.

**Facets Explanation:** Reference answers were verified by crowd annotators on Prolific, who were selected based on demographic criteria (nationality and childhood residence) to match each of the targeted regional cultures. Answering the questions requires declarative cultural knowledge not contained within the question prompt. The benchmark includes 'multi-mode' questions where multiple valid cultural answers are accepted; however, any option failing to reach a strict consensus of at least 4 out of 5 annotators is filtered out.
