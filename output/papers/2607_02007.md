## Benchmark Modalities

**Modality Types:** text, image

**Description:** Combines text-based questions with multimodal Visual Question Answering (VQA) using low-to-medium resolution artwork images.

## Analytical Tasks

**Task Types:** factual recall, multiple choice qa, fact verification, sequence prediction, visual recognition, visual reasoning, interpretation, stylistic analysis

**Detailed Description:** Evaluates art-historical capabilities including factual recall of artists and styles, stylistic analysis, iconographic interpretation, and visual reasoning. Tasks are structured as single and multiple-choice questions, true/false statement verification (fact_verification), in-text word positioning and open/closed fill-in-the-blanks (sequence_prediction), and error identification in art-historical prose.

## Summary

This paper introduces EduArt, an educational-level benchmark containing 871 human-authored questions in Italian and English drawn from secondary school and AP Art History exams. The benchmark uses Classical Test Theory to assess model capabilities across seven diverse formats, and introduces a motivation condition to examine how demanding a written justification (chain-of-thought) affects accuracy.

## Key Findings

Top frontier models achieve near-ceiling performance on multiple-choice items (over 94% accuracy) but suffer severe performance drops on generative formats like open completion (falling to 23.9% for Claude Opus 4.6) and error identification. Controlling for other factors, the presence of images decreased the likelihood of a correct answer. Eliciting written justifications improved performance for Anthropic models but degraded it for Gemini and GPT families.

**Facets Explanation:** The benchmark derives its questions and reference answers directly from established educational assessments, specifically Italian secondary-school exercises (MyZanichelli) and US Advanced Placement (AP) Art History examinations. Correct answers are extracted from the official standardized answer keys of these pedagogical materials, which demand external domain-specific knowledge—such as stylistic classification, provenance, and iconography—not explicitly contained in the provided visual stimuli. Because the evaluation relies on high-school-level curricula with singular, verified ground truths (explicitly avoiding expert-level contested attributions), the paper does not address interpretive plurality or community-dependent variations.
