## Benchmark Modalities

**Modality Types:** text, image

**Description:** A multimodal benchmark combining visual input (images of cultural heritage artifacts) with textual prompts to generate structured textual metadata predictions (JSON format), evaluated against reference metadata via an LLM-as-a-Judge.

## Analytical Tasks

**Task Types:** attribution, classification, cultural reasoning, generation, visual recognition

**Detailed Description:** The benchmark operationalizes structured metadata inference as: 'attribution' (inferring creator, historical period, and geographic origin), 'classification' (identifying cultural region and object functional type), 'cultural_reasoning' (connecting visual style to historical and cultural contexts), 'visual_recognition' (identifying coarse visual features and functional categories of artifacts), and 'generation' (producing structured JSON outputs representing the predicted fields).

## Summary

Appear2Meaning is a cross-cultural multimodal benchmark designed to evaluate vision-language models on their ability to infer structured cultural metadata from images of heritage artifacts. By targeting non-observable attributes like historical period, geographic origin, and creator rather than surface visual descriptions, the benchmark tests the models' latent art-historical and cultural reasoning using an LLM-as-a-Judge evaluation framework.

## Key Findings

All tested VLMs show exceptionally low exact-match accuracy (0.01 to 0.04) for jointly predicting all metadata fields, indicating multi-attribute cultural inference remains a major challenge. However, partial match rates are much higher (up to 0.658), demonstrating that models capture fragmented cultural signals but suffer systematically from temporal compression, stylistic over-anchoring, and cross-cultural misattribution (especially projecting non-Western objects onto Eurocentric categories).

**Facets Explanation:** The dataset derives its ground truth from pre-existing museum metadata from the Getty and the Metropolitan Museum of Art, focusing on the genesis of the artifacts such as their creator, period, and origin. Predicting these correctly requires domain-specific historical knowledge not observable in the visual stimulus alone. Divergence is treated as noise, as the curation process explicitly excludes ambiguous records and utilizes a two-stage human verification pipeline where only objects with verified metadata are retained. For evaluation, an LLM-as-a-Judge scores model predictions on a three-tier correctness scale (correct, partially correct, incorrect) rather than relying on exact string matching against a single key.
