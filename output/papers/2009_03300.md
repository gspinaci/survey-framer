## Summary

This paper introduces the Massive Multitask Language Understanding (MMLU) benchmark, designed to evaluate a model's academic and professional capabilities across 57 diverse subjects. The test is constructed to assess pretraining knowledge exclusively in zero-shot and few-shot settings, shifting the focus of NLP evaluation from basic commonsense to specialized, domain-specific academic knowledge.

## Key Findings

The largest GPT-3 model (175B) achieved an average few-shot accuracy of 43.9%, substantially above random chance (25%) but far below the estimated human expert baseline (~89.8%). Highly procedural STEM subjects and value-aligned subjects like moral scenarios and professional law proved to be particularly difficult for LLMs, and models demonstrated poor calibration.

**Facets Explanation:** The reference answers were obtained directly from pre-existing answer keys of standardized practice exams (such as the GRE, AP exams, and USMLE), undergraduate course materials, and academic textbooks. Because these multiple-choice questions evaluate factual, procedural, and academic knowledge in a closed-book format, correct answers require external domain knowledge not found in the question stimulus itself. The paper does not address annotator disagreement or plurality of readings, relying instead on the absolute correctness of the authoritative source keys.

**Results Explanation:** The paper estimates that 'expert-level accuracy is approximately 89.8%'. The best-performing model evaluated is UnifiedQA which 'attains 48.9% accuracy' (Table 1), resulting in an expert-to-model gap of 40.9 percentage points (rounded to 41). While the paper notes that removing the '</s>' format token from UnifiedQA's inputs causes accuracy to decline 'by several percentage points' (Appendix A.3), it does not quantify this gap precisely or test alternative answer formats (e.g., open-ended vs multiple choice), so no format gap is reported.
