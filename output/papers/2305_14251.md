## Benchmark Modalities

**Modality Types:** text

**Description:** Text-only evaluation that analyzes generated biographies and verifies them against Wikipedia as a reference corpus.

## Analytical Tasks

**Task Types:** factual recall, fact verification, generation

**Detailed Description:** The benchmark evaluates long-form biography 'generation' by prompting the model to generate biographies, requiring 'factual_recall' of biographical details, and then decomposing the generated text into short, single-assertion statements for fine-grained 'fact_verification' against a knowledge source.

## Summary

This paper introduces FActScore, a fine-grained evaluation framework that breaks long-form LLM-generated texts (specifically biographies) into distinct atomic facts and verifies each against a reliable knowledge source like Wikipedia. To mitigate the high cost of human evaluation, the authors develop an accurate, automated retrieval-augmented LLM evaluator. The framework is used to benchmark several state-of-the-art commercial and open-source LMs, demonstrating its utility and cost-effectiveness.

## Key Findings

State-of-the-art LLMs struggle with long-form factuality; ChatGPT only achieves a 58.3% FActScore, which drops dramatically for rare entities. GPT-4 and ChatGPT are significantly more factual than public open-source models, although open models like Vicuna and Alpaca are the best within their size class. The automated estimator closely approximates human judgments with an error rate of under 2%.

**Facets Explanation:** The reference factuality labels (Supported, Not-supported, Irrelevant) were produced by professional freelancers hired through Upwork. Answering the prompt 'Tell me a bio of <entity>' inherently requires external biographical knowledge not present in the stimulus, and annotators verified generated claims against English Wikipedia. Annotator disagreements were analyzed qualitatively on a 10% sample to understand edge cases and inherent debatability, but divergence was ultimately treated as noise, as the evaluation metric relies on a single, unified binary decision per atomic fact.
