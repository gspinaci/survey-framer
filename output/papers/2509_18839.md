## Benchmark Modalities

**Modality Types:** text, image

**Description:** Multimodal classification task pairing historical religious painting images with text prompts containing class names, detailed iconographic descriptions, and few-shot exemplars.

## Analytical Tasks

**Task Types:** classification, visual recognition, interpretation

**Detailed Description:** Single-label image classification of religious paintings representing Christian saints. The task relies on the visual recognition of characters and symbols, combined with the iconographic interpretation of attributes mapped to the Iconclass system.

## Summary

This study evaluates the capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) on the single-label image classification of Christian iconography. Using subsets of the ArtDL, ICONCLASS, and Wikidata datasets, the authors benchmark zero-shot and few-shot performance under various prompt configurations, including plain labels and rich iconographic descriptions.

## Key Findings

General-purpose multimodal LLMs, particularly Gemini 2.5 Pro and GPT-4o, generally outperformed fine-tuned supervised ResNet50 baselines on curated datasets, peaking at over 90% accuracy on ArtDL. However, contrastive models like SigLIP-SO400M performed better on the less consistent Wikidata dataset. Enriching prompts with iconographic descriptions typically improved zero-shot accuracy, while few-shot prompting with five exemplars yielded inconsistent results and often degraded performance.

**Facets Explanation:** The benchmark derives its reference answers from existing curated metadata natively mapped to Iconclass standards across ArtDL, the ICONCLASS AI Test Set, and Wikidata. Identifying the specific Christian saints depicted requires external knowledge of iconographic traditions and symbols that are not explicitly described in the image stimulus itself. The evaluation expects a single correct class label per image—having explicitly filtered out multi-label entries featuring multiple saints—and does not discuss the possibility of diverging annotator interpretations.
