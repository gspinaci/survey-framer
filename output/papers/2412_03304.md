## Benchmark Modalities

**Modality Types:** text

**Description:** Text-only multiple-choice questions across 57 subjects translated into 42 languages, supplemented with cultural sensitivity metadata.

## Analytical Tasks

**Task Types:** factual recall, multiple choice qa, cultural reasoning, logical and quantitative reasoning, normative judgment

**Detailed Description:** Tests multiple-choice question answering demanding factual recall across 57 subjects, including logical and quantitative reasoning in STEM topics. It also evaluates cultural reasoning on a Culturally-Sensitive subset requiring culture-specific or geographic knowledge, and normative judgment on Moral Scenarios and Business Ethics.

## Summary

This paper introduces Global-MMLU, a multilingual expansion of the MMLU dataset translated into 42 languages with professional and community annotations. The authors categorize items into Culturally-Sensitive and Culturally-Agnostic subsets to analyze cultural and geographic biases, demonstrating how translated English benchmarks distort multilingual model evaluation rankings.

## Key Findings

The authors found that 28% of MMLU questions require culturally sensitive knowledge, with 86.5% being Western-centric. Evaluating models on culturally-sensitive vs. culturally-agnostic subsets independently exposes severe rank volatility across the evaluated LLMs, showing an average of 7.3 position shifts on culturally-sensitive subsets compared to 3.7 on agnostic subsets.

**Facets Explanation:** Global-MMLU adapts the pre-existing MMLU dataset, which derives its ground truth from academic exam and quiz answer keys. Answering these questions correctly requires substantial external domain knowledge not contained in the stimulus. Despite being translated into 42 languages and categorized into culturally sensitive and agnostic subsets, the benchmark maintains a single universal correct answer per item across all languages. Disagreements among the human annotators who labeled the cultural sensitivity of the questions were resolved using majority voting, effectively treating divergence as noise.

**Results Explanation:** The paper reports no quantified human baseline, format gap, or single headline score for the best-performing model, presenting results primarily across models in charts. It explicitly confirms a systematic cultural asymmetry, finding that "progress on MMLU depends heavily on learning Western-centric concepts, with 28% of all questions requiring culturally sensitive knowledge." Furthermore, the authors state that "performance declines significantly for low-resource languages" when evaluating multilingual capabilities.
