## Benchmark Modalities

**Modality Types:** text, image

**Description:** Multimodal evaluation pairing historical artwork images with free-text generative expert bilingual critiques across eight cultural traditions.

## Analytical Tasks

**Task Types:** generation, interpretation, attribution, cultural reasoning, stylistic analysis, visual recognition, visual reasoning

**Detailed Description:** Evaluates generative art critique across five hierarchical layers: visual perception (L1), technical analysis of medium and brushwork (L2), decoding of cultural and iconographical symbolism (L3), historical attribution and context (L4), and culture-specific philosophical aesthetics (L5).

## Summary

VULCA-BENCH is a multicultural art-critique benchmark of 7,410 image-critique pairs across eight traditions, designed to test vision-language models' deep cultural and philosophical understanding beyond surface-level perception. Grounded in a five-layer framework mapping from visual features to philosophical aesthetics, it enforces a 'Cultural Symmetry' principle to balance structural depth and representation across Western and non-Western traditions.

## Key Findings

All evaluated VLMs show a prominent 25-40 percentage-point decline (layer gap) from visual perception (L1-L2) to cultural and aesthetic interpretation (L3-L5). Naive few-shot prompting degrades performance across most models, indicating that simple in-context learning is insufficient to elicit deep cultural reasoning.

**Facets Explanation:** The reference critiques and dimension tags were generated by domain-expert annotators, with all annotator-reviewer disagreements resolved through consensus discussion to establish a single gold standard, thereby treating divergence as noise. Correctly interpreting the artworks at layers L3-L5 requires external historical, cultural, and philosophical knowledge not contained in the visual stimuli alone. The artworks span multiple traditions, but correctness for each item is universally tied to its specific cultural origin rather than varying by observer community. While the benchmark encompasses historical context, it targets comprehensive cultural and aesthetic interpretation rather than solely focusing on genesis. The generative task is evaluated in the pilot using a Dimension Coverage Rate (DCR) via keyword matching, but the paper delegates the comprehensive evaluation framework to a companion study, leaving the definitive answer multiplicity unspecified.
