## Benchmark Modalities

**Modality Types:** text, image

**Description:** Combines natural language context sentences with static generated images (Subtask A) or three-panel sequential image narratives (Subtask B) depicting literal or idiomatic meanings. A text-only track replaces images with descriptive captions.

## Analytical Tasks

**Task Types:** multiple choice qa, classification, retrieval and ranking, sequence prediction, interpretation

**Detailed Description:** Tasks require ranking five candidate images or captions based on their relevance to a literal or figurative context (retrieval_and_ranking), selecting the correct image to complete a comic-style narrative sequence (multiple_choice_qa, sequence_prediction), distinguishing between literal and idiomatic meanings of nominal compounds (classification), and interpreting complex figurative semantics (interpretation).

## Summary

The AdMIRe benchmark (SemEval-2025 Task 1) evaluates the ability of large language and vision-language models to interpret nominal compounds in literal and idiomatic contexts. Provided in English and Brazilian Portuguese, it features tasks for ranking static images and predicting appropriate completions for visual-temporal image sequences. The results demonstrate that while top ensembled systems approach human-level performance, foundational models struggle with inherent biases toward treating these compounds as uniformly idiomatic regardless of context.

## Key Findings

Top-performing systems achieved near human-level accuracy (e.g., up to 0.93 Top 1 Accuracy in English Subtask A) by employing mixture-of-experts ensembles, chain-of-thought reasoning, and synthetic data augmentation. However, models demonstrated a strong bias toward idiomatic interpretations and struggled to identify literal uses, heavily depending on complex prompt engineering or multi-step processing pipelines to mitigate these biases.
