## Benchmark Modalities

**Modality Types:** text, image, audio, video

**Description:** Combines text with a wide array of historical sources including printed texts, digitized manuscript scans, historical maps, inscriptions, oral history audio recordings, and documentary video clips.

## Analytical Tasks

**Task Types:** factual recall, multiple choice qa, transcription and correction, translation, causal and temporal reasoning, interpretation, attribution, visual recognition, visual reasoning, cultural reasoning

**Detailed Description:** Evaluates historical scholarship tasks including factual investigation, multi-step temporal/causal inference, optical character recognition/repair of historical manuscripts, translation of ancient and low-resource languages, iconographic interpretation, visual identification of figures/objects, cultural contextualization of sources, and the dating/attribution of epigraphic monuments.

## Summary

This paper introduces HistBench, a comprehensive benchmark containing 414 high-quality, expert-reviewed questions across 29 languages designed to evaluate AI's historical reasoning capabilities under real-world research constraints. The authors also present HistAgent, a domain-specialized AI agent equipped with modular tools for manuscript OCR, multi-step literature retrieval, translation, and image provenance search designed to mirror the workflow of human historians.

## Key Findings

HistAgent based on GPT-4o achieved 27.54% pass@1 and 36.47% pass@2 accuracy on HistBench, significantly outperforming GPT-4o with web search (18.60% pass@1) and open Deep Research agents (20.29% pass@1). HistAgent also demonstrated robust out-of-domain generalization, scoring 60.00% accuracy on the GAIA validation set and 28.57% on the HLE History subset.

**Facets Explanation:** HistBench's 414 reference answers were authored by over 40 expert contributors (including history professors, PhD students, and research assistants) using a standardized protocol and verified through a multi-stage review pipeline. Answering the questions requires substantial historical background knowledge external to the provided visual or textual stimuli, such as ancient language translation and specialized historical context. The evaluation relies on a single gold standard (either a single correct option for multiple-choice questions or a short-form exact match), and the paper does not discuss how inter-annotator disagreement or divergent historical interpretations were handled.
