**Detailed Description:** Evaluates models on factual recall of Indian history, arts, and governance, logical reasoning, and cultural reasoning of region-specific concepts and traditions via multiple-choice questions.

## Summary

This paper introduces MILU, a comprehensive multi-task benchmark containing approximately 79K multiple-choice questions across 11 Indic languages and English. It focuses on evaluating both general and culturally specific knowledge (such as local arts, history, and law) sourced from Indian competitive examinations. The massive evaluation of 42+ models highlights significant performance gaps in culturally specific domains compared to STEM.

## Key Findings

GPT-4o achieved the highest overall performance with 74.74% average accuracy. Open multilingual models consistently outperformed language-specific fine-tuned models, which barely exceeded random baselines. Additionally, models performed significantly worse in culturally relevant domains like Arts & Humanities compared to standard STEM subjects.

**Facets Explanation:** The reference answers for MILU were sourced directly from pre-existing answer keys of over 1,500 competitive and civil service exams across India. Because these are multiple-choice questions testing domain knowledge, answering them successfully requires retrieval of external factual and cultural information not contained within the question prompt itself. Correctness is determined by matching a single gold option, and the paper does not address annotator or key disagreement.

**Results Explanation:** The benchmark reports no performance comparison across different answer formats for the same models. No human baseline is evaluated or quantified for the benchmark. The highest overall performance is achieved by 'GPT-4o', which emerges as the strongest model with an 'average accuracy of 74.74%'. A systematic cultural asymmetry is reported as a core finding, with the authors noting that 'models perform poorly in culturally relevant areas, such as Arts & Humanities and Social Sciences, compared to more general fields like STEM' and perform better in 'high-resource languages as compared to low-resource ones.'
