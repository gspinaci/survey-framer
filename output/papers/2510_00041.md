## Benchmark Modalities

**Modality Types:** text, image

**Description:** Multimodal visual question answering combining comic panel images with textual questions and translation prompts.

## Analytical Tasks

**Task Types:** visual recognition, multiple choice qa, open ended qa, cultural reasoning, translation

**Detailed Description:** The benchmark evaluates visual recognition of culture-specific objects, multiple-choice identification of background cultures, open-ended identification and description of cultural conflicts (using a constrained template), and multilingual machine translation of comic dialogues across five languages.

## Summary

This paper introduces C3B (Comics Cross-Cultural Benchmark), a dataset containing 2,220 comic panels and 18,789 QA pairs designed to assess the multimodal cultural awareness of LLMs. By using comic scenes where multiple cultures can coexist or conflict within a single frame, the benchmark evaluates models across three progressive tasks: culture-aware object extraction, cultural-conflict detection, and multilingual translation of comic dialogue.

## Key Findings

Qwen2.5-VL achieved the strongest overall performance, leading in cultural identification (53.7% ACC) and conflict reasoning (34.2% CACC). However, all models exhibited severe difficulties with complex multi-step reasoning and lesser-known cultures, resulting in a substantial performance gap compared to humans, who achieved 100% accuracy on identifying cultural conflicts.

## Reviewer Notes

- Humanities role: Adjudicated to 'both' because the paper provides positive evidence of distinct item groups (1,197 real images for translation vs 1,023 generated images for cultural conflict) and distinct metrics (BLEU/COMET vs ACC/CACC) to evaluate generic translation and domain-specific cultural reasoning independently. Analytical tasks: Dropped 'generation' from Ciop's list as task 3 is exclusively 'translation' and task 4 operates as a highly constrained 'open_ended_qa' task rather than free-text generation. ASJC Areas: Selected 'Cultural Studies' over 'Literature and Literary Theory' to better match the paper's explicit focus on cross-cultural analysis and conflict.
- The analysts disagreed on answer_multiplicity. Ciop chose 'answer_set', likely because Q1 allows multiple options to be selected simultaneously; however, the ground truth constitutes a single combination (e.g., 'C D') that the model must output, and the translation task provides a single reference sentence evaluated via BLEU. Thus, it was resolved to 'single_answer'. No other disagreements occurred.

**Facets Explanation:** Reference answers were primarily generated using multi-agent pipelines powered by DeepSeek-V3 and DeepSeek-R1, followed by manual verification by student annotators. Correctly identifying cultures, objects, and conflicts in the comic panels requires extensive background knowledge about world cultures that is not explicitly present in the image stimuli. The benchmark treats answers as universally valid rather than community-dependent, and evaluates them against a single reference key or translation per item using exact match Accuracy and BLEU. During the manual curation phase, inter-annotator disagreements were resolved through joint review and consensus group discussions, treating divergence as noise.
