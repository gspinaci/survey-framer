## Benchmark Modalities

**Modality Types:** text

**Description:** The dataset is entirely text-based, consisting of scenario-based questions, answers, and explanations presented in the local languages of 51 regions across 14 languages, alongside English translations.

## Analytical Tasks

**Task Types:** factual recall, multiple choice qa, open ended qa, logical and quantitative reasoning, causal and temporal reasoning, interpretation, cultural reasoning

**Detailed Description:** Tasks include factual recall of cultural norms and regional regulations, multiple-choice and short open-ended question-answering formats, temporal reasoning (e.g., chronological ordering of songs), interpretation of figurative language and symbols (e.g., local proverbs, symbolic meanings of flowers), and complex multi-step logical reasoning grounded in specific cultural scenarios.

## Summary

CulturALL is a multilingual and multicultural benchmark comprising 2,610 evaluation items across 14 languages and 51 regions. It focuses on assessing LLMs on grounded, scenario-based everyday tasks that require local language comprehension, cultural knowledge, and contextual reasoning. The authors demonstrate that current state-of-the-art models struggle significantly on these culturally grounded scenarios.

## Key Findings

The highest-performing configuration (Gemini 2.5 Pro utilizing advanced reasoning and web search) achieves only 44.48% accuracy. Open-source models lag behind significantly, with the best reaching 23.68%. External web search provides a substantial performance boost for commercial models but fails to assist open-source models, and models perform significantly better (an 8.08% improvement on average) when prompted in the task's native language rather than in English translation.

## Reviewer Notes

- gold_source: Ciop chose model_generated, while Cip chose domain_expert. The text describes a mixed methodology where LLMs drafted some items and humans drafted others, but all items were ultimately reviewed and completed by 15 native-speaker 'volunteer annotators'. Although the paper refers to them as 'expert annotators' due to their linguistic and cultural background, they lack specified disciplinary training in the humanities, making crowd_annotation the most accurate fit for the controlled vocabulary. The mix of drafting methods is documented in the explanation.

**Facets Explanation:** The benchmark relies on a human-AI collaborative framework where reference answers are a mix of model-generated drafts (based on existing datasets and web resources) and human-authored items (from personal experience and cross-lingual inspiration). Ultimately, all items and answers were reviewed, refined, and finalized by 15 native-speaker volunteer annotators. Correctly answering the queries requires external, domain-specific cultural knowledge that is explicitly withheld from the context scenarios. The questions are community-dependent, heavily tied to the specific cultural norms of 51 distinct regions. During the peer-review quality control stage, any annotator disagreements or quality issues were treated as noise and resolved by revising or rejecting the items.
